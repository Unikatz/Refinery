# Blameless Failure & Experiment Ledger
> **Learner Safety Rule:** Logging a failed experiment here is a high-value deliverable, not a penalty. Every invalidated hypothesis saves compute and sharpens our next cycle. Read the last 5 entries before starting any new cycle.

## Schema for New Entries
### [YYYY-MM-DD | Cycle ID] - Short Title
- **Hypothesis:** What we believed would solve the human need.
- **Breaking Point:** Why it failed (within the 3-strike circuit breaker).
- **Human-Centric Lesson:** What this taught us about user friction, trust, or cognitive load.
- **Next Pivot:** What the next agent cycle should try instead.

### [2026-11-17 | C001-link-forward] - On-device link reader's "nothing wrong" card read as an all-clear
- **Hypothesis:** Seeing a link's true destination in one plain sentence is enough to help a person decide not to tap (Red Flag Run; static offline page, paste box standing in for share input; rules for owning name, shorteners, raw numbers, the @ trick, lookalike letters and nine brand names; red "Be careful" or muted "Can't confirm: Nothing obviously wrong").
- **Breaking Point:** Built without tripping the breaker and passed 30 fixtures whose rules and expected answers came from the same source; failed at challenge (Cognitive Load 2, Error Recovery 2, Resource & Privacy 4; headless Chromium at 360px, offline, no real phone). Realistic scam links with no listed brand got the same muted card as genuine sites, so the commonest result carried no information and, beside red cards, read as "this is fine". "I couldn't find a link" was a second all-clear (bare links on unlisted endings, wa.me / tel: links, scams with no link). The destination was misnamed for shared hosting and unlisted two-part endings. Honest things were accused: real sign-in links with a return address, every non-Latin address, plain words like "GOV.UK". Journey failed as in C002 (six to seven actions across two apps versus three to four for Forward; no share target or delivery path built), plus a new hazard: in email a short press on the link opens it. Recovery: stale clipboard re-reported as current (as in C002), paste silently overwrote the box, the clipboard-refused note was cut off and covered by the keyboard, "what to do next" fell below the fold from about 1.3x text.
- **Human-Centric Lesson:** A tool that warns only sometimes teaches that no warning means safe; with no reputation data a device-only reader cannot judge, so any verdict layer, including "no link found", becomes reassurance. False accusations and misnamed destinations spend the trust the red card needs. Fixtures written alongside the rules confirm the rules, not the need; the spike's own flags (brandless scam links pass; the page cannot know who a message claims to be from) were the breaking point and were visible before challenge. Held up: zero network requests, nothing stored, 16 KB, pasted content inert, plain and correct red cards for the @ trick, raw numbers and lookalikes.
- **Next Pivot:** Try Stage 1's option C: no link checking; identify who the message claims to be from and give the route that avoids the link, or a rule carried without a tool ("never use the link; open the app or type the address yourself"). If a reader continues: strip verdicts, never reassure when no link is found, name destinations correctly (full public-suffix list, honest shared-hosting wording) or cut the claim, start from the Share menu with one-time set-up by someone else, and prove delivery on a real cheap Android phone. Still open: the check on the owner's phone of whether messaging apps can share a text message to other apps. Option B (forward to a chat contact) stays unverified: accounts, possible cost, a privacy decision, possible overlap with a national service.

### [2026-11-17 | C002-family-guard] - "Ask first" page was slower than the phone's own Forward
- **Hypothesis:** A parent who feels even slight doubt will ask a trusted person if asking takes two taps and the wording is already written (static offline page: paste or type the worrying message, get a ready "can you look at it?" note to share). Reframed from "adult children protect parents remotely" to avoid surveillance.
- **Breaking Point:** Built without tripping the 3-strike breaker; failed at challenge (Cognitive Load 3, Error Recovery 3, Resource & Privacy 4; headless Chromium at 360px, no real phone). "Two taps" counted only taps inside the page: the real journey is about nine actions across three apps versus three or four for native Forward with no app switch. It also assumed long-press-and-copy (fallback: retype the scam); wrong taps destroyed work ("Start again" with no undo, Back and reload lost the note); a stale clipboard went into a ready-to-send note unchecked; the calm line and main buttons fell below the fold at larger text sizes; the adult child had no role. How the page reaches the phone was never solved.
- **Human-Centric Lesson:** Count effort from where the person already is (the messaging app) to the finished outcome, and compare it with what they already do; a helper that is slower than the native path adds friction at the moment of doubt. Unsolved delivery is part of the hypothesis, not a later detail. Held up and worth reusing: nothing stored, nothing leaves the device, 8.6 KB, plain blame-free wording, explain-plus-one-button help, pasted content rendered as inert text.
- **Next Pivot:** Start where the message already is: be a destination in the phone's Share menu, or make the message optional (one button that produces a "can you call me?" note). Give the adult child a one-time set-up job (home-screen shortcut and trusted contact, e.g. carried in the link so nothing is stored on a server), with the parent still pressing send. Make wrong taps recoverable and keep the calm line above the fold. Must match or beat native Forward. Before the next challenge, prove on a real cheap Android phone: delivery path (once hosted, a server sees visits unless offline support is added), clipboard prompt, share sheet, keyboard overlap, font scaling. Option C ("money brakes" checklist) stays blocked on verified facts.
